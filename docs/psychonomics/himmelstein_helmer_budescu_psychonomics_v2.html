Randomized Experiments

  • Behavioral research often deals with experiments where we want to test for positive benefits of some kind of experimental treatment
  • Examples:
    • Does a new medication improve mood?
    • Does a new type of therapy improve well-being?
    • Does an anti-smoking campaign increase likelihood of quitting?
    • Does a new math curriculum improve learning? (running example)

Core research design: Randomly assign participants to conditions:

  • Control
  • Treatment

Between Subjects Designs

  • Simplest version of randomized design
  • Core model

\[ \widehat{\text{Math Score}}_i =\; \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\substack{\text{Control} \\ \text{Score}}} + \underbrace{\beta_1 (\text{Treatment}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Treatment} \\ \text{Effect}}} \]

Limitations

  • Statistical power
  • Does not allow for treatment effect to vary as a function of baseline ability (moderation)

Mixed Designs

  • Adds pretest measurement
  • Controlling for baseline score improves power
  • Allows for moderation
    • Do people who start worse at math benefit more from the treatment?

\[ \widehat{\text{Math Score}}_i =\; \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\substack{\text{Control Score} \\ \text{at Mean} \\ \text{Baseline Score}}} + \underbrace{\beta_1 (\text{Treatment}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Treatment Effect} \\ \text{at Mean} \\ \text{Baseline Score}}} \\[2.5em] + \underbrace{\beta_2 (\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Base Score Effect} \\ \text{in Control}}} + \underbrace{\beta_3 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Treatment Effect} \\ \text{per unit Change in Base Score} }} \]

Measurement Reactivity

  • What if the elicitation of the pretest itself acts as a confounding treatment?
    • Example: Exposure to math test is itself a practice opportunity
    • Goal of treatment is not to test how treatment helps pretested students, but how it helps students in general (typically who would not be pretested)
    • Does the act of being pretested affect
      • Treatment of interest?
      • How baseline moderates treatment effects?

\[ \widehat{\text{Math Score}}_i =\; \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\substack{\text{Control Score} \\ \text{No Pretest} \\ \text{at Mean} \\ \text{Baseline Score}}} + \underbrace{\beta_1 (\text{Treatment}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Treatment Effect} \\ \text{No Pretest} \\ \text{at Mean} \\ \text{Baseline Score}}} + \underbrace{\beta_2 (\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Baseline Score Effect} \\ \text{in Control} \\ \text{No Pretest}}} + \underbrace{\beta_3 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Treatment Effect} \\ \text{per unit Change in Baseline Score} \\ \text{No Pretest}}} + \\[2.5em] \underbrace{\beta_4 (\text{Pretest}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Pretest Effect} \\ \text{in Control} \\ \text{at Mean} \\ \text{Baseline Score}}} + \underbrace{\beta_5 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Pretest}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Treatment Effect} \\ \text{from Pretest} \\ \text{at Mean Baseline Score} \\ \text{\textbf{(Measurement Reactivity)}}}} + \underbrace{\beta_6 (\text{Pretest}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Baseline Score Effect} \\ \text{per unit Change in Baseline Score} \\ \text{from Pretest} \\ \text{in Control}}} + \underbrace{\beta_7 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Pretest}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Treatment Effect} \\ \text{per unit Change in Baseline Score} \\ \text{from Pretest} \\ \text{\textbf{(Measurement Reactivity on Moderation)}}}} \]

Solomon (1949) Four Group Design

  • Randomize participants into crossed \(2 \text{ (Treatment) } \times 2 \text{ (Pretest})\) design
  • Remaining Problem: When \(\text{Pretest}_i = 0\), that means \(\text{Baseline}_i =\) NA!
  • Analysis model (Solomon ANOVA):

\[ \widehat{\text{Math Score}}_i =\; \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\substack{\text{Control Score} \\ \text{No Pretest}}} + \underbrace{\beta_1 (\text{Treatment}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Treatment Effect} \\ \text{No Pretest}}} + \underbrace{\beta_4 (\text{Pretest}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Pretest Effect} \\ \text{in Control}}} + \underbrace{\beta_5 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Pretest}_i)}_{\substack{\text{Difference in Treatment Effect} \\ \text{from Pretest} \\ \text{\textbf{(Measurement Reactivity)}}}} \]

  • Less powerful than between subjects design
  • Impossible to capture moderation effects

New Contribution: Planned missing data design

  • Collect auxiliary variables
  • Impute missing \(\text{Baseline}_i\) values
  • Recover power and moderation?

Reactivity as Missing Data

(Conditionally) Missing at Random

\[P(M = 1 \mid Y_{obs}, Y_{mis}) = P(M = 1 \mid Y_{obs})\]

Mapping to our example

  • \(Y_1 = \text{Baseline}_i\) (pretest score)
  • \(Y_2 = \text{Math Score}_i\) (posttest)
  • \(M_1 = 1 - \text{Pretest}_i\)

Measurement reactivity: the posttest depends on whether baseline was measured

\[ P(Y_2 \mid Y_{1, mis}, M_1 = 1) \neq P(Y_2 \mid Y_{1, obs}, M_1 = 0) \]

Which implies:

\[ P(M_1 = 1 \mid Y_2, Y_{1, obs}, Y_{1, mis}) \neq P(M_1 = 1 \mid Y_2, Y_{1, obs}) \]

So we must separately model:

  • \(P(Y_2 \mid Y_{1,obs}, M_1 = 0)\)
  • \(P(Y_2 \mid Y_{1,mis}, M_1 = 1)\) (How???)

Planned missingness: make mechanism (C)MAR by randomizing \(M_1\) independently of everything measured before treatment.

\[ P(M_1 = 1 \mid Y_2, Y_{1, obs}, Y_{1, mis}, \text{Pretest}) = P(M_1 = 1 \mid Y_{1, obs}, Y_2, \text{Pretest}) \]

  • Then: find a way to impute missing \(Y_1\) from pre-treatment information, and model \(P(Y_2 \mid Y_1, M_1)\)

Auxiliary Variables

  • Auxiliary variables (\(A\)): Outside measurement model, but informative about missing \(Y_1\)
  • The joint model factors into two pieces, estimated together:

\[ \begin{aligned} P(Y_2, Y_1 \mid A, \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}) &= \underbrace{P(Y_2 \mid Y_1, \cancel{A}, \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment})}_{\substack{\textbf{Measurement model} \\ (A \text{ carries no additional information about } Y_2)}} \\[0.5em] &\quad \times \underbrace{P(Y_1 \mid A, \cancel{\text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}})}_{\substack{\textbf{Imputation model} \\ (\text{randomization})}} \end{aligned} \]

  • \(P(Y_1 \mid A)\) is the same in both Pretest groups, so the pretested group shows how \(A\) predicts \(Y_1\)
  • Missing (unpretested group) \(Y_1\) are drawn from plausible values given both \(A\) and \(Y_2\):

\[ P(Y_{1,\text{mis}} \mid Y_2, A, \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}) \;\propto\; P(Y_2 \mid Y_1, \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}) \times P(Y_1 \mid A) \]

  • \(A\) must be measured before randomization and strongly related to \(Y_1\)

Simulation Design

\[ \text{Math Score}_i = {} \beta_0 + \beta_1 (\text{Treatment}_i) + \beta_2 (\text{Baseline}_i) + \beta_3 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i) + \beta_4 (\text{Pretest}_i) \\[0.5em] + \underbrace{\beta_5 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Pretest}_i)}_{\text{Manipulated}} + \beta_6 (\text{Pretest}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i) + \underbrace{\beta_7 (\text{Treatment}_i)(\text{Pretest}_i)(\text{Baseline}_i)}_{\text{Manipulated}} + \varepsilon_{i,2} \]

\[ A_{ik} \sim N(0, 1), \qquad \text{Baseline}_i = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} A_{ik} + \varepsilon_{i,1}}{\sqrt{K + \sigma_{B}^2}}, \qquad \varepsilon_{i,1} \sim N(0, \sigma_{B}^2) , \qquad \varepsilon_{i,2} \sim N(0, 1) \]

  • \(N =\) 450, \(K =\) 3 auxiliary variables, \(R^2_{A} =\) 0.33
  • \(P(\text{Pretest}_i = 1) =\) 0.33, \(P(\text{Treatment}_i = 1) =\) 0.5
  • \(\beta_0 = 0,\ \beta_1 = 0.5,\ \beta_2 = 1,\ \beta_3 = -0.25,\ \beta_4 = 0.2,\ \beta_6 = 0\)
    • \(\beta_1\) implies medium treatment effect, \(\beta_3\) implies baseline offsets treatment
  • Crossed: \(\beta_5 \in \{0, -0.5\} \times \beta_7 \in \{0, 0.25\}\)
  • 4 conditions, 200 replications each

Methods Compared

Benchmarks

  • True: Actual measurement model parameters (not directly observable)
  • Between: Between subjects design (no pretest)
  • Mixed: Mixed (pretest-posttest) design
  • Solomon ANOVA: traditional 2×2 analysis of the four-group design (Planned missingness without auxiliary variables, baseline ignored)

Planned missingness With Auxiliary Variables: Baseline observed only when \(\text{Pretest}_i = 1\); three ways to recover it using \(A\)

  • ML: full-information maximum likelihood (mdmb::frm_em)
  • MI: model-based multiple imputation with factored regression (Blimp via rblimp, \(m =\) 100), pooled with Rubin’s rules (mitml)
  • Bayes: fully Bayesian joint model with Baseline as missing data (brms)

Recovery of Treatment

\(\beta_1\): Treatment effect without a pretest, the effect we want to generalize

  • All methods except mixed, which is biased under measurement reactivity, recover treatment

Detecting Reactivity

\(\beta_5\): Treatment \(\times\) Pretest, pretest moderating treatment effect

  • Between and mixed designs cannot detect reactivity, planned missing designs with auxiliary variables do so more powerfully than Solomon

Recovery of Moderation

\(\beta_3\): Treatment moderated by baseline score without a pretest

  • Between and Solomon can’t detect moderation, mixed is biased under measurement reactivity

Moderated Reactivity

\(\beta_7\): Treatment \(\times\) Pretest \(\times\) Baseline, pretest moderating moderation effect

  • Only planned missing designs with auxiliary variables can detect measurement reactivity on moderation (unbiased, but poor power)

Take Home

  • Between subjects designs are well powered for treatment effect but cannot detect reactivity or moderation effects
  • Mixed designs are very risky! Can trigger measurement reactivity, biasing treatment effects
  • Solomon ANOVA can detect measurement reactivity, but less powerfully than planned missing approaches, and cannot detect moderation
  • Planned missing designs with auxiliary variables simultaneously address these problems:
    • Test whether measurement reactivity is present, and if so
    • Recover unbiased treatment effects
    • Permit varying levels of treatment effect by baseline/trait ability (moderation)
      • Power weak at this \(N\) + effect size (more informative \(A\) might help), but recovery unbiased
    • ML (as implemented) less consistent than MI or Bayes

Next Steps

  • Vary \(R^2_A\)
    • How sensitive is the planned missing approach to the informativeness of auxiliary variables?
    • Would more informative auxiliary variables pull it closer to the between subjects design for estimating the treatment effect?
  • Vary \(N\) and allocations across conditions
  • Vary effect sizes of interest \(\beta_1\), \(\beta_3\), \(\beta_5\), \(\beta_6\) and \(\beta_7\)
  • See if ML is fixable
  • Consider assumptions and violations
  • Find real data applications
  • Expand to other designs
    • No reason this is limited to a \(2 \times 2\)
    • Planned missingness can theoretically address measurement reactivity any time its a problem and possible to collect auxiliaries

Appendix: Comparison with Solomon

Drop \(Y_1\) from analysis

(Solomon Four Group Design)

\[ P(Y_2 \mid \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}) \]

Benefits:

  • Treatment effect without pretest (\(\beta_1\))
  • Can test for measurement reactivity (\(\beta_5\))

Limitations:

  • Essentially between subjects analysis
    • Only based on \(\text{Pretest}_i = 0\) subsample
    • No more moderation
    • Lose statistical power vs. between subjects design

Our goal: bring \(Y_1\) back without measuring it directly

\[ P(Y_2 \mid Y_1, \text{Pretest}, \text{Treatment}) \]

Benefits:

  • Estimate full measurement model (\(\beta_0\) – \(\beta_7\))
  • Greater power for treatment and reactivity effects
  • Reintroduces moderation effects with baseline

Limitations

  • How do we estimate \(Y_1\)?
    • Auxiliary variables + imputation model

Appendix: Missing Data Methods

Maximum Likelihood

mdmb::frm_em()

How it works:

  • Imputation and measurement model written as one factored likelihood
  • Estimated with maximum likelihood (EM algorithm)

Inference:

  • NHST

Multiple Imputation

Blimp via rblimp, pooled with mitml

How it works:

  1. Bayesian MCMC draws \(m\) plausible Baselines for each non-pretested participant
  2. Measurement model fit to each completed dataset with OLS
  3. Results pooled with Rubin’s rules

Inference:

  • NHST

Fully Bayesian

brms with mi(), Stan via cmdstanr

How it works:

  • Measurement model and model for Baseline estimated jointly in one model
  • Missing Baselines are treated as parameters with their own posterior
  • Joint likelihood + priors for imputation and measurement = joint posterior

Inference:

  • Bayes

All three use the same information (A, Math Score, and the measurement model), so all three should recover \(\beta_0\)–\(\beta_7\)
